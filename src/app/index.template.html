What is intermittent fasting?

Intermittent fasting (IF) is an eating pattern that alternates planned periods of eating with periods of little or no caloric intake. It focuses mainly on when you eat rather than prescribing a particular food list.
Common approaches include daily time-restricted eating (for example, 16:8), the 5:2 pattern, and alternate-day fasting.
Potential benefits, limits & safety
Potential benefits
Human trials suggest intermittent fasting can help some adults reduce body weight and waist circumference and may improve blood-glucose control, insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and some blood-lipid markers. Effects are usually modest, vary among people and regimens, and are often similar to continuous calorie restriction when total energy intake and weight loss are comparable.
What is still uncertain
Long-term effects on disease prevention, lifespan, cognitive function and the best fasting schedule remain uncertain. Much of the human evidence is short- to medium-term and is concentrated in adults with overweight, obesity or metabolic risk.
Who should be cautious
Fasting is not suitable for everyone. Seek professional advice before fasting if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, underweight, have a current or past eating disorder, diabetes or another medical condition, or take medicines that can be affected by meal timing. Stop the fast and seek appropriate care for severe weakness, fainting, confusion, dehydration, persistent vomiting or other concerning symptoms.
Evidence & sources
  • BMJ 2025 systematic review — 99 randomized trials; intermittent fasting and continuous calorie restriction produced broadly similar weight and cardiometabolic outcomes, with generally small differences between strategies.
  • U.S. National Institute on Aging — overview of intermittent-fasting patterns, potential health effects and uncertainty about long-term outcomes.
  • NIH Research Matters, 2024 — an 8–10 hour eating window produced modest benefits over three months in people with metabolic syndrome; longer studies are needed.
  • NIDDK, 2024 — possible benefits and medication-related safety considerations for people with type 2 diabetes.

Autophagy & fasting science

Not fasting
Human evidence: limited
Autophagy is active at basal levels even when you are not fasting. Human research has not established a universal hour when fasting-induced autophagy “starts.”
0–8 h — early fast; no clock-based autophagy inference.
8–12 h — ketones commonly begin to rise; metabolic switching starts.
12–24 h — metabolic transition progresses; autophagy onset in humans remains unproven.
24–36 h — human autophagy-related markers have been studied; results are mixed and tissue-dependent.
36–48 h — prolonged fasting; a 36 h human muscle study found only modest effects on autophagy mediators.
48 h+ — extended fasting; there is no validated whole-body “autophagy level” that can be calculated from elapsed time.
This indicator is educational, not a measurement of autophagy and not a reason to prolong a fast. Fasting responses vary by tissue, activity, prior diet, glycogen status and other factors.

Scientific basis & sources
  • Gómez-Virgilio et al., 2022 (PubMed) — autophagy is active at basal levels in cells and responds to nutrient stress.
  • de Cabo & Mattson, NEJM 2019 and 2020 correction — human ketone levels begin rising roughly 8–12 h into fasting and increase further by 48 h.
  • Anton et al., 2022 (PubMed) — review concluding that whether an acute fast produces significant increases in human autophagy remains unknown.
  • Chaudhary et al., 2022 (PubMed) — 12–24 h fasting did not show robust activation of autophagy markers in human skeletal muscle.
  • Dethlefsen et al., 2018 (PubMed) — 36 h fasting produced only modest changes in autophagy-related markers in human skeletal muscle, influenced by training status.

How levels work

Levels measure consistency XP, not fasting duration. Earn 10 XP for each completed fast that meets your chosen target and 2 XP for each distinct day you log weight. Fasting beyond your target gives no extra XP.

  • Level 1: 0–49 XP. Level 2: 50–149 XP (100 more XP). Level 3: 150–349 XP (200 more XP). Level 4: 350+ XP.
  • Later levels require more accumulated XP, so they mark sustained habits over time. Levels offer milestones and motivation only; they unlock no features and are not health scores or a reason to fast longer.

About & Help

Install on iPhone / iPad
  1. Open the app's web address in Safari.
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Keep Open as Web App enabled if iOS shows that option, then tap Add.
Install on Android
  1. Open the app's web address in Chrome or another browser that supports PWA installation.
  2. Open the browser menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. Confirm the installation. Fasting Tracker will appear on the Home screen/app launcher and can work offline after its files are cached.
Backup & restore
The app reminds you every 7 days to use Export and save the JSON backup in Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or another secure location. Before an app update, Backup & Update creates an external backup first. Internal recovery snapshots are stored inside the installed web app and are lost if that installation is removed. The JSON file itself is the complete external backup; no companion text file is required. Before reinstalling to change the Home Screen icon, export an external backup; after reinstalling, use Import to restore it.
Exported JSON backups are plain, unencrypted files. Store them somewhere you consider secure.
Privacy & storage
Your fasting and weight records stay in this device's local web-app storage. The hosted website contains only the app files and does not receive your fasting or weight history. Each user who installs the app has separate local data. Clearing browser or website data can remove local records, so keep a recent backup.
Historical time zones
Fasts and weight entries remember the time zone in which they were created, so historical clock times and calendar-day charts do not shift if you later travel to another time zone.
Autophagy indicator
The science card deliberately does not claim that autophagy starts at a fixed hour. Autophagy occurs continuously at a basal level, and current human studies do not support a universal fasting-duration threshold. The card separates well-established metabolic timing from more uncertain autophagy evidence and links to the studies used.
Consistency system
Gamification is optional. A fast that reaches its chosen target earns 10 XP regardless of whether it ends exactly at the target or continues much longer. An excused unavoidable interruption does not break the target streak and does not earn target XP. Weight logging earns 2 XP per distinct calendar day. The system deliberately does not reward extreme fasting duration.

Fasting Tracker is an informational self-tracking tool, not medical advice. It does not determine whether a fasting duration is safe for you and does not recommend extended fasting.
